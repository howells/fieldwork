# Fieldwork's roster holds up, but four gaps need decisions

Fieldwork already covers the strongest providers in most capabilities, and the biggest problems are gaps, not bad entries. The one clearly redundant provider is **Browserbase for `search`**, because Browserbase resells Exa and Parallel at the same or a higher price. The clearest additions, in value order, are **a document-parsing provider** (Reducto first; LlamaParse is a credible but not leading choice), **Zyte for `product`**, **Spider.cloud for `map` and `crawl`**, **You.com for `research`** and **Scrapfly for `read` on protected sites**. The deprecated-endpoint worries in the research notes mostly do not apply, because I checked the adapters and Exa, Firecrawl and Perplexity already target the current endpoints (Exa `/agent/runs`, Firecrawl `/agent`, Perplexity `/v1/agent`). Weak entries are few: SerpApi is last on the one third-party agent benchmark and carries litigation risk, and Firecrawl `research` sits on a Research Preview endpoint. Almost every number below comes from a vendor, often a competitor of the vendor being scored, so treat rankings as directional. All additions need Daniel's sign-off under AGENTS.md, so each is framed as a decision.

## How far to trust the evidence

Three layers of confidence run through this report. The first is **facts read directly from the repo** (which endpoints each adapter calls), which I verified this session. The second is **facts the researchers read on a primary page**, such as Cloudflare's crawl docs, Firecrawl's monitoring docs, LlamaParse's pricing page and the Artificial Analysis leaderboard. The third is **search-summary claims**, which the notes mark "via search summary" and which I flag again where they carry weight. No neutral, peer-reviewed benchmark exists for any capability here.

The nearest thing to an independent search benchmark is Artificial Analysis, whose Search Index averages three question sets while holding the answer model fixed ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/search-api)). As of 19 October 2026 it scores Exa auto and Perplexity at 80, Octen at 77, Parallel advanced and Brave LLM Context at 75, You.com at 74 and Firecrawl at 73 ([leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api)). The second search benchmark, from AIMultiple, discloses that SerpApi is a paying client, and its top four (Brave, Firecrawl, Exa, Parallel) are statistically indistinguishable ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search)). Everything else is vendor-run: Parallel's BrowseComp comparisons ([Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/the-honest-2026-comparison-web-search-apis-for-ai-agents)), Browser Use's stealth benchmark where Browser Use ranks first ([Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/cloud-browsers)), Datalab's parsing benchmark where Datalab ranks first ([OmniParseBench](https://github.com/datalab-to/omni_parse_bench/wiki)) and Zyte's 2021 product-extraction test ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/scrapinghub/product-extraction-benchmark)). Several scraping "independent" boards (Scrapeway, scraping-test) have anonymous operators, so their independence cannot be checked ([Scrapeway](https://www.scrapeway.com/); [scraping-test](https://github.com/scraping-test/web-scraping-api-2026)).

### Search and images: one redundancy, one weak link

On the Artificial Analysis board the top seven providers sit within about 7 points, so the choice among Exa, Perplexity, Parallel, Brave and Firecrawl comes down to price, latency and filters, not quality ([leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api)). **Browserbase Search is redundant.** It sells Parallel Fast at $1 per 1,000 and Advanced at $5, and Exa Auto at $7, which matches Exa's own list price ([Browserbase](https://www.browserbase.com/search); [Exa comparison](https://exa.ai/blog/best-web-search-tools-for-ai-agents)). It adds a vendor hop on indexes Fieldwork already supports directly. The adapter is still needed for `read`, `browser` and `usage`, so the decision is to drop only its `search` operation. Removing a listed capability changes published behaviour, so it needs Daniel's approval.

**SerpApi** ranked last in AIMultiple (12.28 against Brave's 14.89) ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/agentic-search)), and Google sued it under the DMCA in December 2025. On 20 July 2026 the court dismissed the core §1201 claim with prejudice for results without copyrighted content and the rest with leave to amend ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/court-dismisses-googles-dmca-claims-against-serpapi/583033/)). The docket status after mid-August is unconfirmed. SerpApi stays justified because it is the only provider offering Google-vertical breadth and Google's own ranking, which an agent-quality benchmark does not reward. **Firecrawl search** scored 73 but was the slowest per task (57.5 s), and its general web results come from an unnamed upstream provider ([Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/features/search)). It is not redundant, since it returns page markdown inline.

For additions, **You.com** is the best-evidenced: a flat $5 per 1,000, country, language and freshness filters, and a score of 74 on the independent board ([You.com pricing](https://you.com/resources/lower-search-api-cost); [leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/search-api)). **Octen** scored 77 at $9.07 per 1,000 tasks against Exa's $53.17, the best price-to-quality ratio visible, but the researchers did not read its API docs, so confirm them before any commitment. Linkup claims 92 to 94% on SimpleQA Verified with an open harness, yet that is a vendor figure, and the board where Parallel ranks it puts it at 68 against 80 for Exa and Perplexity, which is a rival's board ([Linkup](https://www.linkup.so/blog/tavily-alternative-web-search-api-comparison); [Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/linkup-vs-parallel)). Bing's search APIs were retired on 11 August 2025 and Google's Custom Search JSON API closes on 1 January 2027, so neither is an option ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/microsoft-ends-bing-search-apis-on-august-11-alternative-costs-40-483-more/); [Massive](https://www.joinmassive.com/blog/google-custom-search-api-alternative)).

For `images`, the market is Google-scraping resellers or Brave's own index. Brave offers up to 200 results with the source-page URL per result at the same $5 per 1,000 ([Brave docs](https://api-dashboard.search.brave.com/app/documentation/image-search/responses)). The researchers could not confirm that Tavily and Parallel return images; **the repo shows both adapters implement it** (Parallel via `/v1/images/search`, Tavily via `include_images`), so the README claim stands, though I did not check either against provider docs. No independent image-search quality benchmark exists. Serper is the one credible addition, at $1 per 1,000 and falling to $0.30 at volume, but it scrapes Google just as SerpApi does ([Exa comparison](https://exa.ai/blog/best-web-search-tools-for-ai-agents)).

### Read, extract, product, map and crawl: Firecrawl carries too much

Firecrawl is the sole `product` provider and one of two for `map` and `crawl`, so the weakest evidence in this report sits under the heaviest dependence. Firecrawl is good on ordinary pages (about 89.7 to 90% markdown quality in WebCrawlerAPI's test, run by a competitor) and degrades on protected targets ([WebCrawlerAPI](https://webcrawlerapi.com/benchmarks)). Scrapeway's October 2026 run has it at 81% against Scrapfly's 95%, with 0% on LinkedIn and Instagram, and scraping-test's June run has it at 83.1% ([Scrapeway](https://www.scrapeway.com/); [scraping-test](https://github.com/scraping-test/web-scraping-api-2026)). No test covered Exa, Tavily or Parallel for `read`, so the current roster cannot be ranked against the candidates. No benchmark of JSON-schema `extract` quality was found for anyone.

**Zyte** is the best `product` candidate: a typed product extractor billed only on success at $0.27 to $1.07 per 1,000 per data type ([Zyte docs](https://docs.zyte.com/zyte-api/pricing.html)). In Zyte's own 2021 test it beat Diffbot on price (F1 0.918 against 0.824) and SKU (0.841 against 0.765) ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/scrapinghub/product-extraction-benchmark)). That test is old, vendor-run and small. The researchers could not retrieve Zyte's extraction docs, so whether Zyte returns variants, which Fieldwork's `product` needs, is **unverified**. Diffbot (a $299 per month floor) and Scrapfly (`extraction_model='product'`, 5 credits) are the fallbacks ([Diffbot](https://www.diffbot.com/pricing); [Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/docs/extraction-api/getting-started)).

For `map` and `crawl`, Firecrawl costs $83 per month for 100,000 credits, while Tavily's crawl runs about $2.40 per 1,000 pages ([Firecrawl pricing](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing); [Firecrawl's Tavily post](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/tavily-pricing)). **Spider.cloud** documents `/crawl` and a `/links` URL-discovery endpoint, at roughly $0.0003 and $0.0002 per request, but its speed and cost claims are its own and unverified ([Spider docs](https://spider.cloud/docs/api/); [pricing](https://spider.cloud/guides/pricing-and-plans/)). Its crawl is synchronous or streamed, so the `resume` rule may not apply and the shape needs checking. **Cloudflare Browser Run** has an asynchronous `/crawl` job of up to 100,000 pages with a cursor, but no map endpoint, so under the no-emulation rule it can be listed for `crawl` only, and it does not defeat bot protection ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-rendering/rest-api/crawl-endpoint/)). Olostep has native `/v1/maps`, `/v1/crawls` and `/v1/batches` (up to 10,000 URLs) and is the only candidate for a real `readMany` batch, but its pricing is reseller-reported ([Olostep docs](https://docs.olostep.com/features/maps)). **Scrapfly** is the most consistent performer on hard targets across several runs, so it is the leading `read` and `extract` addition, with the caveat that those runs are not clearly independent ([Scrapeway](https://www.scrapeway.com/)). Jina Reader would add only a cheap single-URL `read`, with roadmap risk after Elastic acquired it on 9 October 2025 ([usagepricing.com](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/jina-ai-2025-10-09-elastic-acquisition)).

### Answer, research, discover and decide: current endpoints, thin competition

I checked the adapters against the deprecation claims in the notes. **Exa `research` already calls `/agent/runs`**, the Agent API that replaced the removed Research API; **Firecrawl calls `/agent`**, not the deprecated v1 `/deep-research`; and **Perplexity calls `/v1/agent`**, the successor to Sonar ([Exa changelog](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog); [Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/api-reference/v1-endpoint/deep-research); [Perplexity pricing](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/getting-started/pricing)). Two residual risks remain. Firecrawl's `/agent` is described as a Research Preview in an August 2026 third-party post, so it is the weakest `research` entry. And Exa marked `deep-reasoning` search legacy on 19 October 2026 ([Exa changelog](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog)); I did not check whether `search` in the adapter sends that type. The notes also doubted that Exa `/answer` exists, since the changelog is silent; the adapter calls it, and I did not verify the endpoint against Exa's docs.

No independent ranking of research APIs exists. Parallel's self-published figures show its Task API at 88 to 94% on BrowseComp across tiers, while Valyu's self-published DRACO table puts Valyu Heavy at 72.7% and Parallel Ultra8x at 51.2%, which shows how much the results depend on who ran them ([Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/best-deep-research-apis); [Valyu](https://www.valyu.ai/blogs/introducing-deepresearch)). **You.com Research API** is the best addition: tiered from $6.50 to $1,200 per 1,000 requests, with public benchmarks and an open-source harness, although its REST job and resume shape was not verified ([You.com](https://about.you.com/resources/the-ydc-research-api-frontier-tier-reaches-a-new-level-of-state-of-the-art-performance)). Gemini Deep Research is strong but "currently doesn't support structured outputs", so the types would have to refuse `schema` for it ([Gemini docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/deep-research)). OpenAI's deep-research models were shut down on 23 July 2026, so do not add it ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). For `answer`, **Linkup** is the only notable absence, but its 94% SimpleQA figure is self-reported and Perplexity scores 77 to 94% on the same test depending on whose harness runs it, so the numbers do not rank anyone ([Linkup](https://www.linkup.so/benchmarks); [Brave](https://brave.com/blog/ai-grounding/)).

For `discover`, Parallel FindAll (still `v1beta`) has one rival, Exa Websets. The only outside test found, by NewsCatcher, favoured Websets on recall (19.6% against 5.5%) at similar precision, though a Parallel-authored article reported it ([Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/exa-vs-parallel-findall)). Websets is billed by subscription ($49 per month for 8,000 credits), which fits a pay-per-call SDK badly, and Exa's changelog never mentions it ([Websets billing](https://websets.exa.ai/websets/billing)). Whether the Agent API offers match-condition semantics is unchecked. The researchers worried that `decide` might be emulated through model-stated confidence. **The repo shows otherwise**: both Parallel and Perplexity call a `/v1/decisions` endpoint, and Perplexity's adapter parses returned probabilities. The researchers' searches found no such endpoint documented, so verify it against each provider's docs before the README leans on it.

### Browser, act, parse and monitor

For `browser`, Browser Use's stealth benchmark puts its own cloud at 90.5%, Anchor at 82%, Kernel at 80.5% and Browserbase at 78.5%, but it chose sites where its product historically succeeded and says so ([Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/cloud-browsers)). The session-lifecycle test quoted there (BrowserArena, which Browser Use says it does not run) gives Kernel 318 ms against Browserbase's 877 ms. **Kernel** is the leading addition on speed, with managed auth and pooled browsers per its own page, which the researchers could not verify against API references ([Kernel](https://www.kernel.sh/ai-library/browserbase-alternatives-2026)). Cloudflare is cheap but "uses zero stealth", per a search summary I did not see on a primary page. The key `act` decision is definitional: Firecrawl's `act` runs a script, and the closest native matches are Kernel's Playwright Execution API and Browserbase Functions, whereas Browser Use, Airtop and Hyperbrowser take natural-language goals billed in LLM tokens or steps ([Browserbase docs](https://docs.browserbase.com/welcome/introduction)). Daniel should decide whether `act` means script, instruction or both before any provider is added.

For `parse`, the only board that includes all the candidates was written by Datalab, which also ranks first on it: Datalab accurate 94.1, Reducto 90.8 ($10 per 1,000 pages), LlamaParse 90.4 (at the $56.25 Agentic Plus tier), Mistral OCR 89.5 ($4) and Azure 83.0 ([OmniParseBench](https://github.com/datalab-to/omni_parse_bench/wiki)). **LlamaParse is sound but not the leader.** Its cheapest Markdown-capable tier is Cost-effective at $3.75 per 1,000 pages, because the Fast tier returns no Markdown, and the tier that matched Reducto's score costs 15 times more per page than Reducto's flat rate ([LlamaParse pricing](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/llamaparse/general/pricing/); [getting started](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/python/cloud/llamaparse/getting_started/)). Its job-id flow (upload, create, poll) satisfies the `resume` rule but needs a three-call adapter. **Reducto** has the widest documented format list, flat pricing and an async `/parse_async` job (per search results, not read directly) ([Reducto pricing](https://reducto.ai/pricing)). Mistral OCR 4 is cheapest but has narrower documented formats and no documented job id ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)). Datalab has the cleanest async API and the top score on its own benchmark, so treat it as a candidate pending a neutral run ([Datalab docs](https://documentation.datalab.to/docs/welcome/api)). Firecrawl's `/v2/parse` costs 1 credit per PDF page and has no published accuracy data ([Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/features/document-parsing)). General LLMs score well on the board but cost 5 to 10 times more, and would be emulation, so exclude them.

For `monitor`, Parallel (query watching, GA 7 May 2026), Exa (query watching) and Firecrawl (page and search targets, 5-minute minimum) are the right category leaders ([Parallel](https://parallel.ai/blog/monitor-api); [Firecrawl docs](https://docs.firecrawl.dev/features/monitoring)). Exa launched Monitors v2 at $2 per 1,000 runs, down from $15, per a news summary ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/exa-slashes-monitors-v2-web-search-costs-by-87-for-ai-agents)). The adapter calls `/monitors`, which I did not verify matches v2 rather than the older Websets monitors, so recheck it. Visualping documents a real API since April 2026 and is the only credible page-change addition, but its plan pricing conflicts across sources ([Visualping](https://visualping.io/blog/top-ai-website-monitoring-tools)).

## Decisions for Daniel, in priority order

The order weighs how thin the current coverage is, how well the evidence supports the pick and how cleanly the provider fits the no-emulation and `resume` rules.

1. **Add a `parse` provider, Reducto first.** Fieldwork has one provider with no accuracy data. Reducto has the best format coverage and a flat price, and it matches LlamaParse on score at a fifth of the cost of the tier that scored. Adding LlamaParse as second is reasonable for its tier control, and Mistral for price. A small test on real documents (a scan, a handwritten page, a multi-column PDF, a wide spreadsheet) would settle it but needs live-call approval.
2. **Add Zyte for `product`, after confirming it returns variants.** `product` has a single provider that loses protected sites.
3. **Add Spider for `map` and `crawl`, and consider Cloudflare for `crawl` only.** Check Spider's response shape and whether `resume` can apply. Defer Olostep unless a real batch `readMany` is wanted.
4. **Add You.com for `research`, and for `search` at a flat $5 per 1,000.** Verify its job and resume shape first. Octen is worth a docs check.
5. **Add Scrapfly for `read` and `extract` on protected sites.** Its lead rests on non-independent runs.
6. **Add Kernel for `browser`, after settling what `act` means.**
7. **Drop Browserbase's `search` operation.** It is a pure reseller. Weigh it against the cost of a published-behaviour change.
8. **Lower priority:** Serper for `images`, Linkup for `answer`, Visualping for `monitor`, Exa Websets for `discover` (the subscription pricing is the blocker).

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that the roster's problems are concentration, not obsolescence. Firecrawl alone fills `product`, `act` and `parse`, and shares `map` and `crawl` with one other provider, yet it posts its weakest numbers on exactly the protected-site and extraction work those capabilities depend on. The deprecations that the research notes feared had mostly been absorbed already, which means the notes' search-driven view of the market was partly stale relative to the code, and this report's value lies in the cross-check.

Two caveats should shape any build decision. First, the evidence for every addition is vendor-run, so the cheapest safe step before committing engineering time is a private 20 to 50 case eval on Fieldwork's own targets (Eden AI recommends the same, because of benchmark contamination) ([Eden AI](https://www.edenai.co/post/best-deep-research-apis)). That eval needs live calls and Daniel's sign-off, within the $5 cap. Second, several items rest on unverified provider docs: Zyte's variants, Spider's and Cloudflare's response schemas, You.com's job shape, the `/v1/decisions` endpoints and Exa's `/answer` and v2 monitors. None of these checks needs a live call.
